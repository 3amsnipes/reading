Wow, this book was really dope. I had first put this book on pause because I just finished S.A. Cosby’s *All the Sinners Bleed* and I felt it was a bit unfair to read another book—that was fairly similar—directly after reading it. The comparisons would have been too direct and unfair to judge this book for what it is and what it attempted to be. 

I am glad that I took that route. 

Reading this book after as a fresh palette cleanse and especially after reading a few mid to decent books—and some straight up trash (I’m looking at you *My Sister’s a Serial Killer*), this book was a great read and phenomenal way to close out this month of reading.

The main character *Darren* a Black Texas ranger, felt every bit of that throughout the novel. 

But the main things I picked up in this book weren’t even about Darren specifically but the overall writing ability of Attica. I absolutely adored the way she wrote dialogue. Where Keigo Higashino masterfully plugs in so much of the plot into dialogue, Attica plugs in constant prose and internal interjection between each line. You really felt in Darren’s head and you really felt like you were in Texas. I loved this dialogue writing and it was so beautifully done, it was so many quotables and highlights throughout this book as I read through it. 

The actual deduction and mystery was decent, what Darren lacked in amazing critical thinking skills—compared to the likes of Sherlock, Monk, etc.—he made up for in determination and connection with people. He took the time throughout this book to get to know the people of this small town. Tow connect with them and learn them. Shoot when he first got into town he didn’t even announce he was a Ranger—although technically in that moment he wasn’t a ranger, so maybe I’m giving him too much credit—he questioned the people and tried to get to know them. Ultimately his passion for these people—his ability to see them as people, to resonate and have empathy—was the through-line that pushed him to solve this crime. 

The only only negatives I can think of were the multiple crimes and overall formatting as they were jumping through timelines. There would be times where a character—usually Darren, but not always—told a story in the past that was really flushed out, I’m talking a full page sometimes up to three, and then it would cut back to real-time and it would not be easy to keep up with. It didn’t happen often enough to ruin the book completely but it was annoying because it did happen more than once. The congruence of 3 crimes at once—2 in the present, 1 in the past—also made some of the plot and clash of characters confusing at times. The end and overall wrapping of all the crimes made up for this a lot though. I was not at all dissappointed with the plot. it wasn’t anything overly clever—which was sort of the point—but it was done really well. I kind of wish we got more of an ending that Darren was in control of—talking the killer down in a masterful way, plugging all the pieces sooner or more cleverly—but that’s being extremely nit picky and almost asking the book to be something that it is not. 

Overall I enjoyed this book a lot. I’m definitely going to be looking into more of Attica’s books and I look forward to reading the next book in this particular series.
