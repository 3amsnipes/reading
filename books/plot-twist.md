I really enjoyed this book. Harlan's straight forward approach and interweaving his real life stories throughout this was pretty dope. A lot resonated and a lot to ponder on. 


Below are a few notes and quotes that stuck out to me. 

Everything you see is a potential writing prompt
Everything you experience should make you ask what if 

The writers mindset means you’re always mulling on a story

Everyone has hopes and dreams. What are your characters? Have they changed over the years? Do they feel accomplished? Unfulfilled? 

Write down all of ideas, yes even the “bad” ones
One idea isn’t good enough, use multiple.

Knowing the ending allows you to be more comfortable taking detours during the writing

Character and plot are intertwined 
Plot is character, character is plot. 

"Imagine you are standing in front of your character’s home. Take a good long look. What does the house look like? What’s the curb appeal? Are we talking Colonial or McMansion or Second Empire Victorian or split-level? Is it large or small? Does your character live in a house or an apartment or trailer or hotel room or, in the case of Wilde in The Boy from the Woods, an Ecocapsule, which is a metallic egg-shaped micro-home for off-the-grid living? What does that home tell you about your character?

Harlan Coben. Plot Twist (p. 136). (Function). Kindle Edition. "

Ask why now?
Why does this specific murder take place at this time

To create separation from you and your character, give them something you want, and vice versa.

Don't talk about your writing to anyone, it gives you a dopamine hit that is not helpful to your production

“I don’t like writing, I like having written”

To move the story forward all "what's next?" Create mini mysteries that pull the reader in.

"Too many writers think that they should raise the question and withhold the answer. They do this out of fear that they won’t have any more ideas. Be brave. Challenge yourself to up the ante. The best way, the fairest way, is to answer those questions and let that lead to more questions."

Another technique to keep the plot moving is to see your scene from every character’s viewpoint.

Don’t let your fictional choices paralyze you either. Make a choice. It’ll be fine. A book never turns out exactly as you planned.

Create an idea file/notebook

The point is, story is king. Everything should inspire, if not influence, you—books, movies, television, paintings, sculpture, and even cheesy, underrated pop songs.
